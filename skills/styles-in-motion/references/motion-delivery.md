# The sample, and the film

Two things get looked at: the sample before the film is built, and the film before it is called done. Both are looked
at with `aicut look` and read as pictures, not as intentions.

## The sample (about 20 seconds)

Four to six beats, 18–22 seconds, and it must contain the mechanism. A sample that is a title, three stills pushed
and pulled, or a decorative loop has not shown the style explaining anything, and the person will approve the wrong
thing.

It is the film's opening, not a separate object: same file (`film.html`), same page, same rules — so approving it is
approving the film's body, and expanding it is more beats rather than a rebuild.

Check before showing it:

- **The mechanism moves.** The thing that explains (a line drawing itself, a wash flooding, a counter, a camera
  travelling through scale) has to change over time and change the meaning with it.
- **The style's invariants are all there**, each one visible in a frame you actually looked at.
- **Every word is inside the frame and readable** at the size it plays, for the time it is up. Long lines break where
  the language breaks, not where the box ends.
- **Nothing overlaps by accident.** Look at the densest frame, not the emptiest.
- **The first frame has a picture.** A page an editor opens at `t = 0` shows something — a fade-in from nothing leaves
  the film blank where the person clicks.
- **It ends on purpose.** The last beat resolves; it does not stop mid-move.
- **Sound, if any, lands on the beats** and does not arrive late.

Show it, and say in a few lines which part of the film it is, what the sound does, and what is still missing. Then
wait. Approval of the sample is not implied by a chosen style, and a remark about one detail (“颜色可以”) is not
approval of the whole thing.

## The film

The approved sample's code and rules stay; the rest is written in them. Length follows the content: a 40-second
explainer with 9 beats is fine, and so is 2 minutes — do not pad or trim to hit a number.

Look at it in passes, and say what you looked at:

1. **Whole timeline** — every beat, in order, at the length it plays. Does the argument still read with no sound?
2. **Facts** — every number, name and claim; anything uncertain was checked or removed, never smoothed over.
3. **Continuity** — the same object keeps its identity, size and colour across beats; the camera's moves join; a
   transition is a move, not a cut to black unless the style cuts to black.
4. **Readability** — the smallest text, the busiest frame, the fastest beat.
5. **Sound** — the voice (if any) does not fight the music; the mix does not clip; silence where silence is the point.
6. **Ending** — the last beat is the one the film was building to.

Fix what failed and look again. Do not report a film as finished on the strength of the sample, and do not report a
frame as right that you have not seen.

## Delivering

- Say what the film is and how long it is, in a couple of lines.
- Name the style it is in (`nameZh` and `name`), and that the style came from the archive.
- Say what a person can do next: change the style, change a beat, export.
- Export (MP4) only when asked. The film is the page; an export is a copy of it.
